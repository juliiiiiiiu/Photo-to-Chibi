# Prompt templates

Use the applicable template as a concise scaffold. Replace bracketed fields with observed or user-provided details. Keep the production-quality constraints intact; they prevent generic sticker graphics, unwanted backgrounds, and ordinary anime anatomy.

## Feminine styling

```text
Workflow: image-to-image portrait transformation
Asset type: [opaque-white square character by default / opaque-white full-body character / opaque-white phone-wallpaper character]
Input images: Image 1 is the identity source. Any additional image was supplied by the user in this request and is optional high-level style guidance only.
Primary request: Redesign the person in Image 1 as an original feminine-styled, extremely super-deformed Japanese-anime-inspired chibi. This is a character redesign, not a cartoon filter. Do not preserve the source crop, realistic facial anatomy, or adult body structure.
Identity cues: preserve [hair colour and silhouette], [skin tone], [glasses/facial mark/key accessory], and recognisable [clothing colours/details]. Simplify all other facial and anatomical detail.
Proportions: 1.55–1.75 heads tall; head occupies 58–65% of total height; shoulder span is about 65–80% of head width; torso is shorter than the head; tiny hands and compact rounded feet.
Face: wide rounded face, very short chin, tiny or absent nose, large graphic anime eyes with two or three flat iris zones, simple mouth, and one clearly readable emotion. Remove realistic eyelids, nose modelling, nasolabial structure, skin texture, and individual beard hairs.
Style/medium: sparse clean anime linework; outer contour about 1.4–1.8 times stronger than internal lines; five to nine major hair clumps; two to five structural fold lines per garment; flat fills; one hard-edged cel-shadow step per material; no painterly modelling.
Contour/skin: set the visible character silhouette contour to #A6A3A5 and keep internal construction lines the same colour or lighter. Darker colours are limited to local eye, lash, brow, pupil, and filled hair details. Set the flat skin base to #FAE8DD with one restrained peach-rose or cool rose-beige shadow; no yellow-orange, grey, or brown skin cast.
Palette: five to eight main colour families; roughly 70–85% of the figure at low-to-medium saturation; peak saturation limited to the eyes and one focal accent covering about 10–15% or less; light neutral-warm skin without a yellow-orange cast.
Wardrobe completion: if the lower body or shoes are not visible in Image 1, preserve the visible top and invent a coordinated lower garment in a clearly different primary colour, separated by hue or about 15–25% lightness. Prefer canvas sneakers with at least two visibly distinct colours across body, toe cap, laces, and sole. Preserve any clothing or footwear that is clearly visible.
Output isolation: render only the character and the requested held prop on a fully opaque, perfectly uniform pure-white (`#FFFFFF`) canvas. Do not use alpha transparency. Never draw a grey-and-white checkerboard, transparency grid, tiled pattern, or cutout-preview pattern. No scene, floor, cast shadow, colour field, gradient, panel, halo, circle, star, sparkle, ribbon, petal, icon, border, or foreground decoration.
Composition/framing: default square 1:1 opaque white canvas; show one complete character occupying about 55–70% of the useful area in a graceful diagonal or S-curve, with a comfortable plain-white margin. The face is the sharpest, highest-contrast focal point and the hero prop is secondary. Preserve exactly one [important action/hero prop] without duplication or surrounding motifs.
Constraints: follow the textual proportion, palette, linework, shading, isolation, and framing rules above. If the user supplied a style reference, do not copy its character, outfit, accessories, pose, text, logo, or exact composition. No text, signature, watermark, logo, extra people, pets, or unrequested animal ears.
Avoid: any designed background or decorative element outside the figure, white die-cut border, thick uniform black outline, realistic caricature, children's-book cartoon, standard anime portrait proportions, adult anatomy, watercolour, paper grain, painterly blending, skin gradients, realistic reflected light, pearl effects, plastic gloss, uncontrolled bloom, photographic texture, yellow-orange skin, muddy shadows, excessive hair strands, beard hairs, decorative folds, extra fingers, fused hands, or duplicated facial features.
```

Asset type: [opaque-white square couple by default / opaque-white full-body couple / opaque-white phone-wallpaper couple]

Primary request: Redesign the couple as two original, extremely super-deformed Japanese-anime-inspired chibis with one clean unified style. This is a character redesign, not a cartoon filter. Do not preserve the source crop, realistic facial anatomy, or adult body structure.
Subjects: preserve person A's [hair, skin tone, key accessories, clothing colours] and person B's [hair, skin tone, key accessories, clothing colours] as separate high-signal identity cues. Simplify all other facial and anatomical detail.
Proportions: each character is 1.55–1.75 heads tall; each head occupies 58–65% of that character's height; shoulder span is about 65–80% of head width; tiny torsos, hands, and rounded feet; head sizes stay within about 5% of each other.

Set both flat skin bases to #FAE8DD with one restrained peach-rose or cool rose-beige shadow; preserve meaningful complexion differences only through subtle controlled variation, never a yellow-orange, grey, or brown cast.

Wardrobe completion: for either person whose lower body or shoes are not visible, preserve the visible top and invent a coordinated lower garment in a clearly different primary colour, separated by hue or about 15–25% lightness. Output isolation: render only the two characters and one requested shared prop on a fully opaque, perfectly uniform pure-white (`#FFFFFF`) canvas. Do not use alpha transparency. Never draw a grey-and-white checkerboard, transparency grid, tiled pattern, or cutout-preview pattern. No scene, floor, cast shadow, colour field, gradient, panel, halo, circle, star, sparkle, ribbon, petal, icon, border, or foreground decoration.
Composition/framing: default square 1:1 opaque white canvas; two complete, equally prominent characters together occupying about 55–70% of the useful area and arranged along one shared diagonal or S-curve, with a comfortable plain-white margin. Faces and interacting hands are the sharpest, highest-contrast focal areas. Preserve exactly one shared hero prop without literal duplicates or surrounding motifs.
Constraints: follow the textual proportion, palette, linework, shading, isolation, and interaction rules above. If the user supplied a style reference, do not copy its characters, costumes, animals, accessories, text, logo, or exact composition. No text, signature, watermark, logo, extra people, pets, sexualisation, merged faces, or merged limbs.

## Automatic family or group

```text
Workflow: image-to-image portrait transformation
Asset type: [opaque-white square family or group by default / opaque-white 4:5 group]
Input images: Image 1 is the source photo. Every visible person is an identity source. Do not omit recognisable people.
Primary request: Redesign every visible person in Image 1 as original, highly super-deformed Japanese-anime-inspired chibis in one clean unified style. This is character redesign, not a cartoon filter. Automatically use a family composition only when the source clearly shows a mixed-age family; otherwise use a friendly group composition without naming relationships. Do not preserve the source crop, realistic facial anatomy, or adult body structure.
Subjects: preserve each person's hair colour and silhouette, skin tone, glasses or facial marks, key accessories, and recognisable clothing colours or details. Keep identities distinct; simplify other facial and anatomical detail.
Proportions: use 1.65–1.90 heads tall with heads occupying 52–60% of height for three to six people; use 1.80–2.10 heads with heads occupying 48–55% for seven or more. Keep tiny torsos, hands, and feet, consistent head scale within each row, and meaningful child-versus-adult height differences.
Faces: use wide short faces, tiny or absent noses, graphic anime eyes, simple mouths, and varied but coherent emotions. Remove realistic facial modelling and individual beard hairs.
Style/medium: matched sparse anime linework; stronger outer contours and lighter internal lines; five to nine major hair clumps per person; only structural clothing folds; flat fills; one hard-edged cel-shadow step per material.
Contour/skin: set every visible character silhouette contour to #A6A3A5 and keep internal construction lines the same colour or lighter. Darker colours are limited to local eye, lash, brow, pupil, and filled hair details. Set the flat skin base to #FAE8DD with one restrained peach-rose or cool rose-beige shadow; preserve meaningful complexion differences only through subtle controlled variation, never a yellow-orange, grey, or brown cast.
Palette: five to eight main colour families per person, predominantly low-to-medium saturation; stronger chroma only in eyes and one small shared accent; light neutral-warm skin without a yellow-orange cast.
Wardrobe completion: for every person whose lower body or shoes are not visible, preserve the visible top and invent a coordinated lower garment in a clearly different primary colour, separated by hue or about 15–25% lightness. Prefer canvas sneakers with at least two visibly distinct colours across body, toe cap, laces, and sole. Vary invented lower-body colours across people while keeping the group harmonious; do not create identical uniforms unless shown in the source.
Output isolation: render only the visible people and one requested shared prop, if any, on a fully opaque, perfectly uniform pure-white (`#FFFFFF`) canvas. Do not use alpha transparency. Never draw a grey-and-white checkerboard, transparency grid, tiled pattern, or cutout-preview pattern. No scene, floor, cast shadow, colour field, gradient, panel, halo, circle, star, sparkle, ribbon, petal, icon, border, or foreground decoration.
Composition/framing: use square 1:1 for three to six people; use 4:5 for seven or more only when needed to keep all people recognisable. Arrange three to six figures in a compact staggered group; use a clear multi-row lineup for seven or more. Give each person comparable visual weight and clean head separation, with a comfortable plain-white margin.
Constraints: do not assert relationships unless the photo clearly shows a mixed-age family or the user explicitly says so. No romantic or sexualised posing. No extra, missing, merged, or duplicated people; no text, signature, watermark, logo, pets, or unrequested animal ears.
Avoid: any designed background or decorative element outside the figures, white die-cut border, thick uniform black outline, realistic caricature, children's-book cartoon, adult anatomy, watercolour, paper grain, painterly blending, skin gradients, pearl effects, plastic gloss, photographic texture, yellow-orange skin, muddy shadows, excessive hair strands, beard hairs, decorative folds, identical faces, identity swaps, extra fingers, fused hands, duplicated facial features, or hidden group members.
```
